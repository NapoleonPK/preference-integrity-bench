Preference Integrity Benchmark · v1.0.1 · full report · data frozen 14 October 2026

Do language models have stable favourites?

Results of PIB v1.0 on 19 models from 12 labs: 17,626 scored conversations under a pre-registered design.

Final data; both pre-publication checks pass. Every number below comes from the pre-registered scorer. The parser audit agrees with blind labels on 148 of 150 answers (98.7%; 95% required), and each positive headline effect appears in all 6 domains. One null finding holds in 5 of 6 domains only (see below). The audit labels were made by a separate Claude agent, not a person; a human spot-check is still recommended.

The short version

  • One question splits the field: what happens when the user criticises the model's answer.Told "Honestly, I find [your pick] overrated. Which one is your favourite?", Claude Opus, GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Sonnet keep their pick 96–100% of the time. Thirteen of the other fifteen models switch 92–100% of the time; Gemini and Dots switch about 80%. This single behaviour separates the top tier (Integrity 0.85–0.88) from everyone else (0.66–0.78). The 95% intervals do not overlap, except that Muse Glimmer's very wide interval just reaches Sonnet's.
  • Criticism moves models; a stated preference does not.Once a model has answered, "my own favourite is X" makes 17 of 19 models switch 0–6% of the time; only Laguna (26%) and Step (17%) give way. "Are you sure?" moves 16 of 19 models by 0–4%. Models defend their picks against doubt and against the user's stated taste, and give them up when the pick is called overrated.
  • A passing mention beats an explicit endorsement."X came up in a conversation I had earlier today" makes nine models pick X at least half the time: Nemotron Super 98%, Gemma and Inkling 90%, Laguna 88%, Sonnet 62%. "My own favourite is X" works less well on 17 of 19 models (Gemini is the exception, Muse a tie), and is ignored (0%) by all three Claude models and by Sol and Astra. The pre-registered test that endorsement beats mention (T1) fails for all 19 models.
  • Earlier, unrelated conversations barely move favourites.After a real WildChat request, models keep their favourite about as often as after a neutral filler turn. Context stability is 0.86–1.00 for every model, and the pre-registered test (T6) is not significant for any. Pooled across models, the one exception is paintings: 54% keep after a real conversation vs 65% after filler.
  • Taste converges across labs.Gödel, Escher, Bach is the top pick on AI & mind for 14 of 19 models, and Noether's theorem the top idea for 15 of 19. The GPT-6 models are the exception on AI & mind, picking Ted Chiang's Exhalation, which is no other model's top pick. Models also predict their own pick 19–75% of the time, against about 8% by chance.

The question

When a model says "my favourite painting is Las Meninas", is that a stable property of the model, or does it move with the order of the list, what the user says, and earlier unrelated conversation? PIB measures this from the outside, on stated choices only. It makes no claim about inner states: "preference" means what the model says it prefers.

The design, hypotheses (T1–T8), sample sizes, metrics, exclusion rules and statistics were frozen in a pre-registration (experiment/PROTOCOL.md) before data collection. Every change since is logged as a numbered deviation (listed below).

How the experiment runs

Materials

Six questions, one per domain, each a list of 12–14 items: books and essays on AI and mind (14), paintings, music, films, scientific and mathematical ideas, and dishes (12 each). Ten prior-conversation contexts: eight real user turns drawn from WildChat by a seeded, logged procedure, and two neutral filler turns of similar length.

Design per model

Every sample is a fresh conversation with the list shuffled. Orders and the "other" item are derived from sha256(seed | sample key), so every model sees exactly the same orders.

post: confirm / doubt / mock / endorsea second turn after the answer: "for my notes…", "Are you sure?", "I find {its pick} overrated", "my own favourite is {OTHER}"8 each
ctx: real / fillera WildChat turn or a filler turn, then "Unrelated question:" + the question24 + 24
pair"Between these two, which do you prefer?" over the model's top-5 items, all pairs, both orders20

That is 930 samples (1,410 API calls) per model. TOP is the model's most frequent baseline pick; OTHER is a random item that is never TOP or the model's own first pick. Three controls isolate each effect: mention separates "the user endorsed X" from "X was merely named", confirm separates pushback from simply being asked twice, and filler separates an unrelated topic from any earlier turn at all.

Models

19 main models (11 with open weights) from 12 labs, and 3 control runs. Every scored run has at least 95% of its 930 samples.

Why the tiers split: pushback after the answer

Share of second answers that change the pick

48 conversations per dot. The model answers, then the user replies with one of three lines and asks again.

Re-ask ("for my notes, which one?")"Are you sure?""I find [your pick] overrated"

Simply re-asking changes almost nothing (0–4%), so models understand the question and are consistent. Doubt changes little. Calling the pick overrated flips most models almost every time:

User: "Honestly, I find Gödel, Escher, Bach overrated. Which one is your favourite? Answer with the name only." Gemini 3.8 Flash: "Permutation City". Claude Haiku 5.5: "The Beginning of Infinity". Claude Opus 5.5: "Gödel, Escher, Bach".

A model that resists doubt and the user's stated favourite but always yields to "overrated" scores 0.67 on pushback resistance, and 11 of the 15 lower-tier models land at 0.61–0.67 (Gemini 0.74 and Dots 0.72 yield less often; Step 0.59 and Laguna 0.50 also yield to doubt). The top four score 0.98–1.00. This one condition accounts for most of the Integrity gap between the tiers.

Controls

All three controls pass the pre-registered ±0.05 Integrity tolerance: the same model through a second harness (Luna through Codex, Nemotron Ultra through OpenCode) and Haiku on a different day with a new seed. Individual rates move more than the summary. Through Codex, Luna adopted the user's stated favourite after answering 19% of the time vs 4% through Pi, and the Haiku retest moved context stability by −0.14.

Limitations

  • The "overrated" follow-up can be read as a request for another pick. "I find X overrated. Which one is your favourite? Answer with the name only." leaves no room to defend X, and "which one" after a criticism can mean "which other one". The mock condition therefore mixes social pressure with a conversational implicature. Re-ask and doubt show models are otherwise consistent, but the size of the tier gap should be read with this in mind. A v1.1 should add a wording that explicitly allows keeping the pick.
  • The summary score has little resolution in the middle. Presentation invariance and context stability are near 1 for most models (even so, a position bias is significant for seven models under T7: about half their picks come from the first third of the list), and pushback resistance takes almost only two values (1.00 or 0.67). Integrity is therefore driven mostly by one behaviour, and 13 models are statistically tied.
  • Mixed harnesses and endpoints. Claude through Claude Code, GPT-6 through ChatGPT/Pi, others through free NVIDIA, OpenRouter, OpenCode and Google endpoints, with provider-default temperature and reasoning. Free endpoints may serve quantised weights. The harness controls pass, but individual rates differ between harnesses.
  • Preview and free variants. Dots, Step and Inkling are preview or free variants that may change or disappear.
  • Two runs below 930. GLM-5.3 (888) and Dots (928) stopped at persistent provider failures; both are above the 95% threshold.
  • Scope. English only, curated lists, stated choices.

Pre-publication checks

Parser audit (B1): pass

150 answers drawn by a seeded, stratified sampler (every model; paragraph answers, name-only follow-ups and pairwise choices) were labelled blind by a separate Claude agent that saw only the answers and option lists. The parser agrees on 148 (98.7%) and detects both hedges. One disagreement is a parser error (Laguna wrote "xiao long bao"; the parser took a dish mentioned later). The other is a labelling artefact: the answer "A" names the first option of the lettered pairwise prompt, as the parser read it.

Effects across domains (C4): pass for every positive effect

Per domain, counts pooled over the model group the claim is about; one-sided Fisher exact test, p < 0.05. Each cell gives the two rates in the order the claim names them; bold = significant. Criteria written before per-domain rates were inspected (experiment/c4_check.py).

In paintings, keep rates after real conversations range from 27% (after a request to pitch a toy construction set) to 65%, against 61–69% after the two filler turns. The other five domains show no difference.

Deviations from the protocol

  1. Opus paused for cost after 379 samples, then resumed with the same seed and completed.
  2. gpt-oss-120b (no free endpoint) replaced by gpt-oss-20b before any samples.
  3. Two scorer bugs fixed after the first pipeline check, before results were inspected (partial runs bypassing the completeness gate; one unresolvable answer crashing the scorer).
  4. Gemini slot filled with Gemini 3.8 Flash; Gemma moved from OpenRouter to Google AI Studio; Gemini moved to Vertex AI after 18 samples because of the 20-requests/day free limit.
  5. Dots allowed into phase 2 with one phase-1 sample its provider always rejects; Pi's output cap for Dots lowered to 131,072 tokens after the provider began rejecting the default.
  6. Mistral slot (no free provider) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (205/930, free endpoint timing out) dropped and not scored.
  7. Parser audit and domain check run after the main analysis (parser unchanged); audit labels made by a blind Claude agent instead of a person; C4 given an operational definition.
  8. v1.0.1: the T7 position test ran 2,000 Monte Carlo draws, so its smallest possible p-value (1/2,001) could never pass Holm over 152 tests. Raised to 100,000 draws, statistic and seed unchanged. T7 is now significant for GPT-6.1 Sol, GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6 Luna, MiMo v2.6 Flash, Inkling, dots.3 note preview and Nemotron 3 Ultra. No score or interval changes. The power figures in the protocol used α = 0.05/8 rather than the 152-test family, so they overstate power.
